\subsection*{Research question and empirical context}

The gap and its extensions converge into a single research question:

\begin{quote}
\emph{When the value to be distributed within an energy community is a regulated transfer rather than a market surplus, and regulation itself [-restricts the set of admissible allocations,-]{+defines which allocations are admissible,+} how do allocation mechanisms rank against each other in terms of distributive equity, incentive strength of the rule and economic sustainability of participation; to what extent do different fairness indicators agree in evaluating [-them;-]{+them, and which subset of indicators suffices to discriminate among the mechanisms;+} and to what extent does this ranking depend on the {+size, the prosumer penetration and the+} composition {+by category+} of the community?}
\end{quote}

{+Each term of the question corresponds to a quantity computed by the model. Distributive equity is measured by indicators of uniformity, of proximity to the marginal contribution of each member and of coalition stability, all evaluated on the same allocations. The incentive strength of a rule is measured ex post, as the cross-sectional covariance between the share received by each member and the temporal alignment of that member's profile with the community, normalised on a reference mechanism; it indicates how much a rule rewards synchrony, not how members would respond to it, a behavioural response that lies outside the scope of the model. Economic sustainability is measured by the net present value of the investment of each member, computed with the share of the incentive that each rule attributes to that member. The agreement among indicators is the rank correlation (Kendall's $\tau_b$) between the rankings they induce, and the dependence on the configuration is the frequency with which the ordering of a pair of mechanisms reverses along the size and penetration axes. Admissibility is verified on each allocation against the threshold of the regulatory framework.+}
